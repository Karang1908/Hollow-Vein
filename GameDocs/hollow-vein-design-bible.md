# PART 1 — THE MAP: MERIDIAN CITY

One persistent, open city, built from nine connected districts. Every season reuses and re-contextualizes this same map instead of introducing throwaway levels — by Season 12, players know these streets by heart, which is deliberate.

| District | Description | Seasons Used | Traversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meridian Central (Downtown)** | Glass towers, the Central Bank, financial district rooftops | S1, S8 (rooftop ritual sites) | Streets, rooftop-to-rooftop |
| **The Undercity** | Storm drains, subway tunnels, maintenance shafts running beneath the whole map | S2, S6, S7, connective tissue for S9–12 | Tunnel network, flooded sections |
| **Lowfen** | Dense low-income district, cult territory, narrow alleys and tenement blocks | S4, S8 | Streets, fire escapes, rooftops |
| **Meridian PD HQ & Black-Site Archive** | Government complex on the city's edge, high security | S3 | Interior, restricted-access floors |
| **Dominion Dynamics Compound** | Private military contractor's walled industrial compound | S5, referenced S11 | Perimeter fence, warehouses, motor pool |
| **Old Docks & Harbor** | Cargo cranes, shipping containers, open water | S1 (extraction), S5, S11 | Boats, cargo-crane traversal |
| **The Crater / Crash Site** | Original landing zone, cordoned off since Season 1 | S9, S10, S12 | Descends into the Ember Vault and, finally, Kryptex's chamber |
| **Meridian Regional Airfield** | Small municipal airport at the city's northern edge, mostly decommissioned | S11, S12 evac | Runways, hangars, control tower |
| **The Overpass Network** | Elevated highways stitching every district together | All seasons (fastest ground route) | Cars, motorcycles, hijacked vehicles |

**Talia's Workshop**, a converted subway maintenance depot, sits between Lowfen and the Undercity — it's the team's persistent hub from Season 3 onward (loadouts, lore terminal, and eventually the Ember Vault entrance in Season 10).

# PART 2 — THE PLAYERS

## Breacher
**Fantasy:** the wall between the team and everything trying to kill them.
- **Core kit:** shotgun/heavy weapons, riot shield, breaching charges (forces locked doors instead of needing a key/hack)
- **Unique traversal:** can force open jammed doors, kick down weak walls, drag a downed teammate at full speed instead of a slow crawl
- **Unique utility:** the only role that can hold a chokepoint indefinitely (stance-based aggro tanking) and clear weapon jams on heavy team equipment
- **Growth arc:** starts with a pistol and a crowbar in S1, ends the campaign as the one physically holding/aiming the Starbreaker's core in S12

## Infiltrator
**Fantasy:** the reason the team gets in without a fight at all.
- **Core kit:** suppressed sidearm, lockpick set, hacking rig, smoke/flashbangs
- **Unique traversal:** grapple hook for vertical shortcuts (rooftops, silos, crane cables), can vault fences/windows other roles can't
- **Unique utility:** the only role that can hack terminals, cameras, and (from S5 on) enemy drones/vehicles remotely
- **Growth arc:** starts hacking simple door locks in S1, ends able to override Kryptex's own defense subsystems for a few seconds mid-fight in S12

## Sentinel
**Fantasy:** the reason the team comes home.
- **Core kit:** sidearm, medkit, deployable cover/shield drone, revive tool
- **Unique traversal:** none extra — deliberately the "grounded" role, but moves faster while carrying supplies than other roles do
- **Unique utility:** the only role that can revive a downed teammate (no auto-respawn from S11 on) and the only one who can stabilize a Vein-exposure before it turns someone
- **Growth arc:** starts with a basic medkit in S1, ends channeling the Starbreaker's stabilizing "core" function in S12 — without Sentinel, the weapon overloads

## Analyst
**Fantasy:** the reason the team knows what's coming before it arrives.
- **Core kit:** marksman rifle, recon drone, scanner/marking tool
- **Unique traversal:** can rappel/zipline using scanned anchor points, best pilot stat for aircraft and boats
- **Unique utility:** the only role that can read sonar/thermal, identify enemy weak points before engaging, and (from S9) safely interface with Choir tech without triggering it
- **Growth arc:** starts marking basic enemies in S1, ends being the one who reads Kryptex's "verification" patterns in real time during S12, telling the team exactly when to channel

# PART 3 — CORE MECHANICS

### Movement
- **Walk / Jog / Sprint** — sprinting drains a stamina meter shared with melee and vaulting; running loud, walking quiet.
- **Crouch** — reduces noise and hitbox, required for most stealth sequences and tight vents/crawlspaces.
- **Prone** — full stealth profile, needed to pass under some Undercity pipe sections and to avoid line-of-sight from Sentry Drones and Cipher Sentinels; slowest movement speed.
- **Climb / Vault / Mantle** — context-sensitive on ledges, fences, and low walls; Infiltrator's grapple extends this to rooftops and cranes.
- **Swim** — required in flooded Undercity sections and Harbor missions; stamina-limited, no weapon use while swimming.
- **Ledge-hang / Shimmy** — used in vertical Season 8 rooftop sections.

### Weapon Handling
- **Equip / Holster** — swapping weapons has a brief animation cost, encouraging pre-planning before engagements.
- **Aim-Down-Sights (ADS)** — slows movement, tightens accuracy, required for headshots on armored enemies (Dominion Enforcers).
- **Reload (manual)** — magazine-based, can be caught mid-animation and interrupted, punishing careless reloads in the open.
- **Weapon jams** — heavy weapons (Breacher's kit especially) can jam under sustained fire; only Breacher can clear a jam quickly, others take a long fumble.
- **Melee & finishers** — quick melee for interrupts, stealth finishers on unaware Thralls/guards (Infiltrator fastest, Breacher hits hardest).
- **Throwables** — frag grenades, smoke, flashbangs, and (from S5) EMP charges that disable Sentry Drones and Cipher Sentinels without alerting organics.

### Ground Vehicles
- **Driving (cars, vans)** — arcade-style handling, drift-capable, can ram light barricades and weak enemies; used constantly on the Overpass Network for fast district-to-district travel.
- **Motorcycles** — faster, more maneuverable, no passenger armor — a risk/reward option for solo scouting runs.
- **Hijacking** — Infiltrator can hotwire a vehicle without keys; other roles need keys or force entry (Breacher can just break the window).
- **Boats** — Harbor missions and some Undercity flood-tunnels use small motorboats; Analyst gets a minor handling bonus.

### Aircraft
- **Stealing planes/helicopters** — introduced properly at the Meridian Regional Airfield in Season 11: Infiltrator bypasses the hangar security, Analyst (best pilot stat) takes the stick, and the other two hold the tarmac from Dominion/Vein interference during startup.
- **Flight model** — simplified arcade flight: throttle, pitch/roll/yaw, auto-hover assist near the ground, no full flight-sim complexity — the point is mobility and set-piece spectacle (evac runs, aerial recon, a Season 12 approach to the Crater from above), not a flight sim inside a shooter.
- **Parachute/fast-rope insertion** — used for a couple of "drop into the mission hot" openings once aircraft are unlocked (S11, S12).

### Interaction & Environment
- **Hacking (Infiltrator)** — minigame-style terminal hacks, timed, can be interrupted by patrols.
- **Lockpicking (Infiltrator)** — physical locks, faster than hacking but noisier.
- **Reviving (Sentinel)** — a channel-based action performed in the open, drawing enemy attention while it happens.
- **Crafting (all four, Season 10+)** — Ember Vault forge sequences require all four players contributing simultaneously (matching roles to sockets).
- **Shared meters** — Oxygen (flooded sections), Light (blackout sections), Noise (stealth sections), and Vein-Exposure (a slow corruption meter that rises near heavy infestation and can only be reset by Sentinel) recur across seasons as environmental pressure, not just health bars.

# PART 3.5 — AAA-STANDARD SYSTEMS LAYER

### Enemy AI & Combat Depth
- **AI states:** Idle → Suspicious (investigates a noise/glimpse) → Alert (actively searching, calls for backup) → Combat (full engagement) → Search (loses the team, sweeps last-known position before de-escalating). Stealth runs live or die on this loop, not a binary seen/unseen.
- **Cover system:** context-sensitive snap-to-cover for both players and AI; enemies flank and suppress instead of standing still, forcing players to actually reposition rather than trade shots from one spot.
- **Ballistic model:** per-body-part damage (headshots, limb damage slows enemies), armor penetration tiers (Dominion Enforcer armor needs specific ammo/weak points, not just more bullets), bullet drop on sniper-tier weapons for Analyst at range.
- **Destructible cover:** wooden/drywall cover degrades and eventually breaks under sustained fire; concrete/metal doesn't — teaches players to read the environment instead of trusting all cover equally.
- **Ragdoll & hit reactions:** physics-based death animations, staggered by explosions/melee, environmental knockback (a Breacher shield-bash sends a Thrall into a wall it can visibly collide with).
- **Enrage/adaptive difficulty on bosses:** boss health thresholds trigger new attack patterns (already true of the Broodmother/Kryptex phase structure) plus a soft "team taking too long" enrage timer on Nightmare-tier replays to keep hard-mode runs tense.

### Progression & Customization
- **Per-role skill trees:** each of the four roles has its own branching unlock tree (e.g., Breacher: Juggernaut / Demolitions / Close-Quarters branches) so two players on the same role can build differently.
- **Weapon attachments & modding:** sights, suppressors, extended mags, underbarrel attachments — found or crafted, not just cosmetic, each with a real tradeoff (suppressor = quieter but less damage).
- **Gear/armor loadout screen:** visible on-character equipment, not just stat sheets — armor plates, utility pouches, role-specific gadgets all render on the player model.
- **Talent respec:** free between missions, so squads can adapt their build to a specific season's demands (e.g., stealth-heavy Season 6 vs. combat-heavy Season 11).
- **Vein-Exposure long-term stat:** an optional deeper layer — repeated high exposure across a campaign run subtly unlocks a "corrupted" cosmetic/ability branch late-game, a risk/reward system for players who lean into danger instead of always playing safe.

### Traversal & Physics
- **Full parkour verb set:** vault, mantle, wall-run (short bursts, Infiltrator-favored), ledge-shimmy, rope/zipline traversal at specific set-pieces (crane cables in the Docks, rooftop lines in Lowfen).
- **Environmental interactivity:** breakable doors/windows beyond scripted breach points, climbable debris piles, swimmable flooded rooms that weren't always flooded (dynamic water level in some Undercity sections tied to story state).
- **Weather & day/night cycle:** rain in the Docks affects visibility and footstep noise; night missions (several Lowfen sections) shift AI vision cones and light-mechanic sections.
- **Vehicle damage model:** cars and boats take visible, functional damage — smoking engines slow top speed, blown tires affect handling — not just a health bar.

### Presentation & Feedback
- **Dynamic music system:** layered adaptive score that shifts instrumentation between stealth/combat/boss states in real time rather than hard music cuts.
- **Spatial/3D audio:** directional footsteps, muffled sound underwater or through walls, critical for the sound-stealth seasons (S6, S9) to actually work as intended.
- **HUD scaling & minimap/objective marker system:** squad-shared objective pins, ping system (non-verbal callouts for players without mics), health/stamina/meter UI that clears away during pure exploration beats.
- **Photo Mode:** unlocked after Season 1, for the inevitable screenshots of the four-player squad and boss fights.

### Social & Live-Service Scaffolding
- **Lobby & matchmaking:** role-locked queueing (can't queue without all four roles filled or matchmaking fills the gap), voice comms with role-based callout shortcuts (quick-chat pings: "Reviving!", "Hacking!", "Contact left!").
- **Cross-progression:** account-bound progress across platforms, since GDGoC events likely span multiple devices/dev environments.
- **Checkpoint & save system:** mid-mission checkpoints for the 2–3 hour seasons (nobody replays a full 3-hour mission from scratch after a wipe), full manual save only at safe hub points (Talia's Workshop).
- **Seasonal live-ops calendar:** monthly story drop + weekly grind ops + limited-time remix events, all visible on an in-game calendar/battle-pass-style track (cosmetic rewards only, no pay-to-win, appropriate for a GDGoC showcase project).

### Accessibility & QoL
- **Full remappable controls**, colorblind modes, subtitle/caption sizing and speaker-tagging, difficulty sliders separate from enemy damage (e.g., toggleable aim-assist, stealth-detection leniency) so the campaign is approachable for a dev-community demo audience of mixed skill levels.

## SEASON 1 — "First Contact" *(~1 hr)*
**Map:** Meridian Central (Bank), Old Docks (extraction)
**Flow:**
1. Briefing at Talia's — wait, no Talia yet; briefing via an anonymous tip. Team picks Break In / Inside Job / Standoff.
2. **Break In path:** Breacher forces a side door (breaching mechanic tutorial); Infiltrator disables one camera loop; Sentinel/Analyst hold the perimeter and call callouts.
3. **Inside Job path:** Infiltrator badges the team in; Analyst watches camera feeds for patrol timing; Breacher/Sentinel wait for the signal to move.
4. **Standoff path:** a rival crew already has hostages; Sentinel negotiates/stabilizes tension while Breacher and Infiltrator flank.
5. All paths converge at the vault. The container cracks during the struggle to open it — player-caused, not scripted.
6. Extraction by boat from the Old Docks while the newly-infected NPC flees into the storm drains.

**Role breakdown:** Breacher = forced entry & muscle; Infiltrator = access & cameras; Sentinel = crowd control/negotiation; Analyst = overwatch & timing calls.
**Mechanics taught:** walk/run/crouch, basic weapon handling, lockpicking vs. breaching, driving to the extraction point.
**Enemies:** bank guards, rival human crew (Standoff path only).
**Fragments:** none — the container itself becomes a recognizable Relic later.

## SEASON 2 — "Broodling" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** The Undercity (storm drains → subway maintenance)
**Flow:**
1. Team enters storm drains tracking bio-residue; Analyst deploys first recon drone.
2. Tunnel maze section — Infiltrator navigates blind turns called out by Analyst's sonar; crouch/prone required through low pipe sections.
3. Barricade sequence — Breacher holds a collapsing gate solo while the other three clear a side path; if it falls, the level shifts to a running fight through flooding tunnels (swim mechanic).
4. Boss arena: **the Broodling.** Breacher tanks charges, Infiltrator flanks for openings, Sentinel manages a gas-hazard oxygen meter, Analyst calls the creature's sound-based tells.
5. Extraction back through the tunnels, now partially flooded.

**Role breakdown:** Breacher = barricade + boss tanking; Infiltrator = maze navigation + flanking; Sentinel = oxygen meter + healing; Analyst = sonar calls + tell-reading.
**Mechanics taught:** prone, swim, sonar-based blind navigation, first boss pattern-reading.
**Enemies:** Thralls, first Crawlers.
**Fragments:** first Vein Shard.

## SEASON 3 — "Buried Files" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** Meridian PD HQ & Black-Site Archive
**Flow:**
1. Meet Detective Achebe at a dead drop; briefing on the archive's layout.
2. Team splits: Breacher/Sentinel create a distraction (staged disturbance) to pull a guard patrol; Infiltrator/Analyst infiltrate through a service entrance.
3. Three-terminal hacking sequence, done in parallel — touching any terminal starts a shared lockdown timer, forcing genuine time pressure across both pairs.
4. Firefight against Dominion Enforcers guarding the deepest archive room once the lockdown timer triggers alarms.
5. Extraction via the Overpass Network, high-speed car chase optional side-objective (lose pursuing Dominion vehicles).

**Role breakdown:** Breacher = distraction & rearguard combat; Infiltrator = terminal hacking (lead); Sentinel = distraction & field medic during the firefight; Analyst = terminal hacking (support) + chase navigation.
**Mechanics taught:** parallel objective timers, first armored-human enemy fights (ADS/headshots matter), vehicle chase driving.
**Enemies:** Dominion Enforcers.
**Fragments:** 🔵 Blue Fragment of Knowledge.

## SEASON 4 — "Quarantine" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** Lowfen
**Flow:**
1. Team arrives to a district already in chaos; live choice — quiet evac or public exposure — changes the mission's structure immediately.
2. **Quiet path:** stealth-focused building-by-building evacuation, avoiding cult patrols.
3. **Public path:** loud, chaotic, larger Thrall/Fanatic engagements, more collateral risk, more district trust earned if successful.
4. Either path converges on the cult's shrine — three mid-tier corrupted enemies guard a hidden Shard.
5. Final objective: get evacuated civilians to a safe extraction point under Burster fire.

**Role breakdown:** Breacher = door-to-door forced entries / heavy engagements; Infiltrator = patrol avoidance / lock bypass on locked apartments; Sentinel = triage, stabilizing panicked or lightly-exposed civilians; Analyst = marking Burster positions before they can fire.
**Mechanics taught:** branching mission structure from a single live choice, first proper multi-enemy-type combat.
**Enemies:** Thralls, Bursters, Awakened Fanatics.
**Fragments:** Vein Shard from the shrine.

## SEASON 5 — "Competing Interests" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** Dominion Dynamics Compound, Old Docks
**Flow:**
1. Team splits into two full sub-squads (2+2) for the first time — one shadows Dominion's extraction convoy, one races to a second sample site.
2. Convoy squad: vehicle chase/ambush mechanics, hijacking a Dominion truck mid-drive.
3. Sample-site squad: hacking Sentry Drones instead of destroying them (first EMP use), racing a countdown before Dominion backup arrives.
4. Live choice at convergence: steal Dominion's research, sabotage it, or ally with Major Esparza.
5. Extraction by boat through the Harbor, optional firefight with pursuing Enforcers.

**Role breakdown:** Breacher = convoy assault; Infiltrator = drone hacking + truck hijack; Sentinel = keeping both squads' pace realistic (reviving under fire, managing the countdown pressure); Analyst = marking convoy vehicles and coordinating both squads over comms.
**Mechanics taught:** true simultaneous two-squad missions, vehicle hijacking mid-chase, EMP throwables.
**Enemies:** Dominion Enforcers, first Sentry Drones.
**Fragments:** 🔴 Red Fragment of Will.

## SEASON 6 — "The Hive Beneath" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** Deep Undercity
**Flow:**
1. Full-mission stealth descent — sustained noise draws hive swarms, making this the strictest stealth season yet.
2. Analyst on constant sonar duty, whisper-calling routes; prone/crouch mandatory through most corridors.
3. Mid-mission: discovery of Detective Achebe, fused into the hive wall — a scripted, unskippable story beat, but the team must still fight off a Crawler ambush to reach her.
4. Hive Construct gauntlet — stationary turret-like growths force route planning instead of open combat.
5. Retreat sequence once the hive "wakes" fully — sustained running combat back to the entrance.

**Role breakdown:** Breacher = point-of-contact combat during the retreat; Infiltrator = quiet route-clearing; Sentinel = keeping the team's noise-linked stress/exposure meter in check; Analyst = sole navigator via sonar for 80% of the level.
**Mechanics taught:** sustained stealth under pressure, environmental-hazard combat (fixed-position enemies).
**Enemies:** Crawlers (paired), Hive Constructs.
**Fragments:** Vein Shard cluster (largest single non-boss drop so far).

## SEASON 7 — "Queen's Wake" *(2–3 hrs, mid-boss)*
**Map:** Undercity boss arena, city-wide blackout overlay
**Flow:**
1. Approach to the Broodmother's chamber during the citywide blackout triggered by its emergence.
2. Light-sharing mechanic: only one light source exists, role rotates as it's passed/dropped under pressure.
3. Boss fight: Breacher tanks charge attacks, Analyst calls weak points revealed only in light, Sentinel manages poison-spore phases, Infiltrator flanks during recovery windows.
4. Post-kill: the hive fragments and scatters — cutscene shows three new district hive-nodes lighting up on the city map, setting up future seasons.

**Role breakdown:** see above — this is the first boss with a true rotating-role mechanic (light bearer) instead of fixed roles.
**Mechanics taught:** shared light-meter management, phase-based boss combat.
**Enemies:** the Broodmother (boss) + Crawler/Burster adds.
**Fragments:** 🟡 Yellow Fragment of Light.

## SEASON 8 — "The Awakening Rite" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** Lowfen rooftops & streets, four simultaneous ritual sites
**Flow:**
1. Team splits fully — one player per ritual site, radio-only coordination, shared countdown.
2. Breacher's site: a Hive Construct nest defending a rooftop altar — heavy combat solo.
3. Infiltrator's site: rooftop sniper nest requiring stealth traversal via grapple hook.
4. Sentinel's site: street-level swarm requiring careful resource/ammo conservation solo.
5. Analyst's site: Calder's personal guard — a scripted near-boss-tier fight requiring called-in support fire from a teammate who's already finished their site.
6. Countdown resolves — ritual disrupted or (on failure) partially succeeds, affecting Season 9's opening state.

**Role breakdown:** true 1-vs-1 split for the mission's main body — the only season where all four players are physically alone for an extended stretch.
**Mechanics taught:** solo enemy-composition combat, cross-site radio coordination, calling in remote support fire.
**Enemies:** Awakened Fanatics (full strength), a Hive Construct nest, rooftop snipers, Calder's guard.
**Fragments:** 🟢 Green Fragment of Growth — the Origin Key's final piece.

## SEASON 9 — "Signal" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** The Crater (surface approach)
**Flow:**
1. Talia scene: the four Chroma Fragments assemble into the Origin Key.
2. Approach to the crash site — first appearance of Cipher Sentinels, puzzle-combat hybrids only vulnerable to specific role actions.
3. Transmitter room: live choice (destroy immediately vs. risky extra scan) plays out as a timed combat-and-hack sequence.
4. Extraction under a harder wave if the risky choice was taken.

**Role breakdown:** Infiltrator interfaces with the transmitter safely (Choir-tech affinity established here); Analyst reads the Cipher Sentinels' patterns; Breacher and Sentinel hold the chamber entrance against the wave that spawns once the transmitter is touched.
**Mechanics taught:** puzzle-combat hybrid enemies, safe-interaction-with-alien-tech mechanic (Infiltrator specialization payoff).
**Enemies:** first Cipher Sentinels.
**Fragments:** Origin Key completed; two bonus Vein Shards.

## SEASON 10 — "Dig In" *(2–3 hrs, prep season)*
**Map:** The Crater → Ember Vault; city-wide (fortification sub-missions)
**Flow:**
1. Four-socket door sequence at the Vault entrance — all four players slot their Chroma Fragment simultaneously.
2. Inside the Vault: meet Talia properly, forge tutorial (crafting mechanic introduced).
3. City fortification sub-missions (players choose which districts to reinforce, splittable 2+2 or full team depending on chosen scope) — resource-gathering combat against Cipher Sentinels and leftover hive-nodes.
4. Recruitment scenes — Achebe and/or Esparza can rejoin depending on earlier season outcomes.
5. Talia's warning: not enough Shards to finish the Starbreaker — grind loop opens.

**Role breakdown:** all four equal partners in the door sequence (deliberate symmetry moment); otherwise mission splits according to player choice on which fortification priorities matter most.
**Mechanics taught:** crafting sequence, strategic district-priority choices with real stat consequences for Season 11.
**Enemies:** Cipher Sentinels, Thrall/Crawler defense waves.
**Fragments:** forge unlocked, partial Starbreaker built.

## SEASON 11 — "Siege" *(2–3 hrs)*
**Map:** Full city, culminating at Meridian Regional Airfield
**Flow:**
1. Multi-front wave-defense across whichever districts were fortified (or weren't) in Season 10 — direct consequence payoff.
2. No-auto-respawn revive mechanic in full effect — a downed teammate must be physically reached.
3. Mid-mission: Airfield sequence — Infiltrator bypasses hangar security, Analyst takes the stick, Breacher/Sentinel hold the tarmac against a Dominion/Vein incursion during aircraft startup.
4. Aerial sequence: fly to intercept the scout-class bio-construct before it reaches downtown.
5. Boss fight: the scout, first live-fire test of the half-built Starbreaker.

**Role breakdown:** Infiltrator = hangar security bypass; Analyst = pilot; Breacher/Sentinel = tarmac defense; all four = coordinated Starbreaker test-fire on the scout.
**Mechanics taught:** full aircraft mechanic (stealing + flying), true no-auto-respawn stakes, first co-op weapon-channeling mechanic.
**Enemies:** full roster wave (Thralls, Crawlers, Bursters, Sentry Drones, Cipher Sentinels), scout-construct boss.
**Fragments:** largest Shard cache in the game.

---

## SEASON 12 — "Origin" *(3+ hrs, finale)*
**Map:** The Crater → Kryptex's chamber (deepest point of the map, only accessible now)
**Flow:**
1. Full descent through the transformed crash site — callbacks to every prior Undercity/Crater visit.
2. Phase 1 (echoes Season 7): light/dark mechanic, Kryptex only vulnerable when illuminated.
3. Phase 2 (echoes Season 6): sound-stealth phase, Kryptex hunts by noise between attack patterns.
4. Phase 3 (echoes Season 8): split-squad phase, team separated across four points of Kryptex's chamber simultaneously.
5. Final phase: the Starbreaker only fires with all four players channeling into it at once — first and only time in the whole campaign this exact "all four, same action" beat occurs.
6. Kryptex's verification sequence, death, rupture, and the closing scene with Talia and the Origin Key.

**Role breakdown:** every mechanic from Seasons 1–11 gets reused here in sequence, each tied to the role that specialized in it, before the final phase where all four roles perform the same action together.
**Mechanics taught:** none new — this season is entirely payoff, no new mechanic introduced, deliberately.
**Enemies:** Kryptex (final boss), supported by its own Cipher Sentinel constructs in early phases.
**Fragments:** none needed — the Starbreaker becomes a permanent legendary weapon for replays.

# PART 5 — THE COMPLETE STORY, END TO END

A meteorite that isn't a meteorite lands outside Meridian City — a seed, sent deliberately by an ancient, long-vanished race called the Choir, left behind as a failsafe against something they were running from. A routine bank heist accidentally cracks its containment, and a bystander is infected with what the city will come to call the Vein: a black-gold, spreading, half-intelligent growth that doesn't just kill — it remembers, and rebuilds, and learns.

Over the following months, a four-person team chases the outbreak through the city's underside — a fast, feral first mutation in the storm drains; proof, buried in a government archive, that the authorities knew this was coming and let it happen anyway; a private military contractor already moving to weaponize whatever the Vein turns out to be; a whole district gone dark under a prophet who believes the growth is a god shedding its skin. Every answer they find raises the stakes instead of lowering them, and every fight leaves behind a fragment of something older than the outbreak itself — cut crystal, in blue, red, yellow, and green, clearly built by the same hands that made the Vein, clearly built to do something about it.

Deep in a coordinated hive beneath the city, the team learns the Vein isn't just growing anymore — it's listening, learning the shape of the people trying to stop it. They kill its matured Queen-stage in a citywide blackout, only to watch it fragment and scatter instead of dying clean. A cult's desperate ritual to accelerate everything at once nearly succeeds; stopping it costs the team their last quiet fragment, and completes something none of them expected: the four crystal pieces assemble themselves into the Origin Key, a device clearly meant to open a specific door for a specific reason.

That door sits at the original crash site, and behind it is the Choir's own forge — and an old artificer, Talia, who's spent years trying to get it running without knowing why. The Key opens it. Inside, the team learns the truth the whole campaign has been quietly building toward: the seed that landed a year ago wasn't just a failsafe — it was quietly transmitting the entire time, calling for help, and something is finally coming to answer.

A scout arrives first — small enough to fight, large enough to nearly end the city on its own. The team meets it in full siege, using everything they've built: fortified districts, old allies who chose to stand with them, and a half-finished weapon called the Starbreaker, forged from a year of collected fragments, that only works if all four of them commit to it at once. They win, barely, and learn what's still coming.

At the deepest point of the crater, in a cathedral of a year's growth, they finally meet what the Key was always meant to open a door to: not a monster born from infection, but Kryptex — the Choir's original guardian-cipher, woken not by the outbreak but by the Key itself, whose entire purpose is to test whether whoever's holding it actually earned it. The fight draws on everything the team has learned across a year — light, sound, separation, sacrifice — until the only path forward is the one thing they've never done before: all four of them, doing the exact same thing, at the exact same time, channeling everything they have into a weapon built by something that vanished before humanity could write it down.

Kryptex doesn't die fighting. It dies satisfied — verifying, to the last second, that four different people carrying four different burdens chose, in the end, to carry the last one together. It ruptures instead of falling, scattering fine spores across a skyline that's finally quiet for the first time in a year. Talia watches the dust settle and says the only honest thing left to say: it's not gone, it's asleep — and whatever else the Choir was running from, it'll need the same four people, doing the same four jobs, to answer it when it comes.
